What we built
Mars Mission Commander is a browser game that lets young explorers lead a six-turn Mars training
mission. Players explore a 3D outpost, meet their crew, allocate electricity, manage oxygen, water
and food, and choose between science and protection. Each committed decision changes the
mission and receives a short explanation. NASA observations and research appear in contextual
science cards, with clear boundaries between real evidence and fictional game rules.
The problem and the challenge
Space survival is difficult to teach through facts alone: producing oxygen, growing food and
protecting a crew compete for limited resources. Our response to the challenge is a place to
practise those connected decisions. The intended audience is young learners, especially ages 7-12,
with potential use in classrooms, science clubs and family learning. This age range and educational
benefit still require learner testing.

AI-GENERATED DIAGRAM / FICTIONAL GAME RULES
A turn is an abstract planning step, not a measured Martian day. Exploring, reading and revising a plan do not advance the
mission.
1. Arrive and meet the crew. Watch or skip the illustrative Earth-to-Mars arrival. Follow the
destination marker and talk to the crew to begin the guided objectives.
2. Inspect the systems. Visit the power station, life-support unit, water storage, greenhouse and
lab. Each panel introduces one decision and its cost.
3. Prepare a plan. Power or pause oxygen production and growing lights, then choose one crew task:
research, rover sampling, shelter, a shield upgrade, Earth advice or conserving power. Some tasks
unlock later.
4. Compare the forecast. See the projected resource changes and warnings before acting. Revise
an unsafe or unaffordable plan. Only Advance turn commits the choice.
5. Respond to changing conditions. Solar-radiation warnings make protection important. A dust
event lowers solar generation. Rover sampling uses extra water; Earth advice arrives on the next
turn.
6. Review and retry. A debrief compares crew safety, research, samples and reserves. Replay to try
another strategy or use independent mode after understanding the controls.
What counts as success?
Finish six turns with oxygen, water and food above zero, exposure no higher than 12 game points, at
least three research objectives and one field sample. Other outcomes distinguish a safe return
without all science goals from a mission needing rescue. These thresholds are teaching rules, not
astronaut health limits.

What the player can learn

planned separately. The example demonstrates software behaviour; it does not prove improved
learning or real-world mission safety.

Implementation, progress and limits
Working in the local prototype
The current build includes guided arrival and crew conversations, walking and overview cameras,
selectable facilities, resource forecasts, a six-turn mission, radiation and dust events, rover-route
planning and sample collection, delayed Earth hints, save/resume, a debrief and a science library.
Camera and rover routes are illustrative. Water-recycling equipment is visible but does not produce
an extra water resource.
Layer
Tools and purpose
Interface
React and TypeScript: contextual panels, readable dialogue and guided objectives.
3D world
Three.js, React Three Fiber and Drei: models, cameras, lighting and interactions.
Rules and saving
Pure TypeScript calculations; browser local storage; Zod checks save and evidence
structure.
Assets
Blender and Python: original procedural geometry; portable GLB model exports.
Development
Vite and Node.js 24; Vitest tests; Playwright browser automation; ESLint and GitHub
Actions.
Evidence and accessibility
All 44 local unit, data and asset checks passed on 14 November 2026. They cover mission logic,
guidance, exploration and source/model consistency. A documented earlier browser run passed 14
desktop/mobile-emulation checks; the final recovery-message patch has an outstanding browser
rerun. We do not claim a completely verified final browser suite.
Keyboard and touch controls, large interaction targets, reduced-motion support, lower rendering
quality, an alternative 2D mission map and replayable guidance are implemented. Mobile emulation
and automated accessibility checks do not establish real-device performance or full screen-reader
accessibility.
Limits and next steps
The prototype has no explorable building interiors, free rover driving, live space-weather feed or
measured Mars terrain. Crew models share an articulated suit design. Mission timings and balances
are authored, and current game dialogue is text rather than the demo narrator.
Next steps are supervised learner sessions, manual keyboard and screen-reader checks, testing on
ordinary phones and laptops and other browsers, and publishing a stable version. We would
evaluate whether learners can explain one resource trade-off before and after playing, then revise
the guidance. These are planned evaluations, not completed results.

NASA science used in the game
The project stores selected, archived evidence locally. Source records were acquired/reviewed on 13 November
2026; no live NASA API is required while playing.
1. MOXIE: producing oxygen on Mars
Source: NASA/JPL-Caltech, MOXIE mission-completion article, 6 September 2023. The
demonstration aboard Perseverance separated oxygen from atmospheric carbon dioxide. Use: the
life-support explanation connects oxygen production to spending electricity. Boundary: the game
is not a MOXIE performance model; its crew-scale plant, energy costs and oxygen yields are
fictional.
MOXIE mission-completion article
2. RadLab: archived radiation observations
Source: NASA OSDR RadLab, ISS DosTel1 absorbed-dose-rate records from 1 April 2022,
23:00-23:05. A dosimeter measures radiation exposure. Use: four archived readings appear in a
contextual chart to show that radiation changes over time. Boundary: these are ISS readings in
microgray/hour, not Mars measurements, medical risk estimates or game exposure points.

3. OSD-314: plant responses to light and gravity
Source: NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), OSD-314 version 7, and Villacampa et al.
(2021), DOI 10.3390/ijms22020899. Researchers studied Arabidopsis seedlings under controlled
spaceflight and reduced-gravity conditions. Use: the greenhouse lesson explains why lighting
matters and competes for power. Boundary: the game does not analyse raw gene-expression files
or predict edible crop yields. A two-turn harvest is a teaching rule.

Space context and responsible use
4. DONKI: an archived solar warning
Source: NASA CCMC / Moon to Mars Space Weather Analysis Office, DONKI event
2024-05-10T06:27:00-FLR-001. GOES-P EXIS recorded an X3.9 flare on 10 May 2024; its listed peak
was 06:54 UTC. Use: an archived example supports the solar-warning explanation. Boundary: flare
class does not establish radiation dose or arrival at the Mars outpost. The game event schedule and
exposure points are fictional.

Source: image PIA24485, Perseverance Mastcam-Z, acquired 22 February 2021 and added to the

to a fictional rover sample task. It does not define the outpost terrain, route or landing location.

Other agency resource: Canadian Space Agency
The CSA toolkit Food production on the Moon and in remote areas informs the food-planning

How the evidence was prepared
We retained source names, links, record IDs, dates, units, credits and limitations. Four RadLab rows
were stored as structured text data (JSON) without changing their measurement values. OSD-314
metadata and its paper support a bounded explanation. A small NASA image derivative is loaded
locally. Scientific observations remain separate from the game rules; no synthetic NASA dataset or
live forecast is claimed.

Brainstorming and AI/SI disclosure
Miro: our brainstorming and planning workspace
We used Miro to brainstorm and organize the project concept. The board connects the Commander
role, crew introductions, base facilities, starting resources, protection and player decisions. Its
scenario flowchart helped make the relationship between survival, food, research and exploration
visible. We used it as a planning reference, not as scientific data or evidence that every proposed
feature works.
Open the team brainstorming board
The board retains early Moon-base wording and exploratory ideas such as extra zones and repair
activities. The current deliverable is the Mars prototype described here. Those planning notes are
not claims of a playable Moon mission, a repair system or unlocked multi-region exploration. The
current mission rules and observed behaviour define this document.
